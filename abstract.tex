\noindent
\entradaAutor{}. \textit{\englishTitle{}}. \the\year. \pageref{LastPage} f. Undergraduate Thesis (Bachelor's Degree in Computer Engineering) - Instituto Politécnico, Universidade do Estado do Rio de Janeiro, Nova Friburgo, \the\year.
\vspace{\onelineskip}

\setlength{\parindent}{1.3cm}
The failures of the tailings dams in Mariana, in 2015, and in
Brumadinho, in 2019, showed the importance of warning, in time, the
population that lives and/or circulates near tailings dams. This work
developed a safety system for mining tailings dams, based on the
Internet of Things, that combines soil saturation, obtained on site,
with observed and forecast rainfall and issues alerts at three levels,
green, yellow and red, both locally and remotely. The alert model
applies a two-variable hydro-meteorological threshold: the relative
soil saturation, obtained by a hygrometer, represents the cause, and
the rainfall accumulated over 72 hours, made up of 48 observed hours,
from the Brazilian National Meteorological Database (BNDMET), and 24
forecast hours, from OpenWeatherMap, represents the trigger. When both
data are available, the level rises only when both variables reach
their thresholds. When the hygrometer reading or the rainfall data are
missing, contingency rules keep the alert working. When the rainfall
data are incomplete, the system uses the sum of the portions actually
obtained, which is the guaranteed minimum of the accumulated rainfall.
A virtual prototype was built and simulated in Wokwi with an ESP32
microcontroller, and the system was also built as a physical prototype
with the ESP8266, powered through the USB port, both with local alerts
by LEDs and a buzzer. The remote alert is provided by a web platform,
developed with Node.js and TypeScript, PostgreSQL and Next.js, with a
landing page, resident registration, an administrator dashboard and the
sending of alert e-mails about the need to evacuate. The energy
redundancy was sized by calculation to meet the requirement of 24 hours
of supervision and 15 minutes of alarm of alarm system standards, which
led to a factory-assembled pack of lithium-ion cells in parallel
(9500\,mAh minimum and 3.7\,V), with a single module that charges the
battery and powers the system at the same time, and a theoretical
autonomy of about 36 hours in supervision; its
operation was demonstrated only in the simulation. In the 31 test cases
executed, covering the alert model, the contingencies, the energy
supply and the physical prototype, the observed output matched the
expected output, and the alert e-mail was sent to the five residents
registered in the test. The results are limited to a single measurement
site, whose thresholds were not adjusted for that site, to the use of a
sensor that provides only a relative reading of soil moisture and to a
redundant external power supply sized by calculation but not assembled in the
physical prototype. It is concluded that it is possible to build, with
low-cost components and open-source technologies, an alert system that
can support the execution of the emergency plan in the early evacuation of
the population in risk areas of tailings dams.
