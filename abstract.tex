\entradaAutor{}. \textit{\englishTitle{}}. \the\year. \pageref{LastPage} p. Undergraduate Thesis (Bachelor's Degree in Computer Engineering) - Instituto Politécnico, Universidade do Estado do Rio de Janeiro, Nova Friburgo, \the\year.
\end{flushleft}
\vspace{\onelineskip}

\setlength{\parindent}{1.3cm}
The failures of the tailings dams in Mariana, in 2015, and in
Brumadinho, in 2019, showed the importance of warning the downstream
population in time. This work developed a safety system for mining
tailings dams, based on the Internet of Things, that combines soil
saturation, obtained on site, with observed and forecast rainfall and
issues alerts at three levels, green, yellow and red, both locally and
remotely. The alert model applies a two-variable hydro-meteorological
threshold described in the literature: the relative soil saturation,
obtained by a hygrometer, represents the cause, and the rainfall
accumulated over 72 hours, made up of 48 observed hours, from the
Brazilian National Meteorological Database (BNDMET), and 24 forecast hours, from OpenWeatherMap, represents the trigger.
When both data are available, the level rises only when both variables
reach their thresholds, all taken from published studies. Contingency
rules keep the alert working when the hygrometer or the rainfall data
are missing; with incomplete rainfall data, the system uses the
guaranteed minimum of the accumulated rainfall. The system was built as
a physical prototype with the ESP8266 microcontroller, powered through
the USB port, and as a Wokwi simulation with the ESP32, both with local
alerts by LEDs and a buzzer. The remote alert is provided by a web
platform, developed with Node.js and TypeScript, PostgreSQL and
Next.js, with a landing page, resident registration, an administrator
dashboard and the sending of alert e-mails about the need to evacuate.
The energy redundancy was sized by calculation for 24 hours of
supervision and 15 minutes of alarm, a requirement of alarm system
standards, which led to three lithium-ion cells in parallel, and its
operation was demonstrated only in the simulation. In the 31 test cases
executed, covering the alert model, the contingencies, the energy
supply and the physical prototype, the observed output matched the
expected output, and the alert e-mail reached the five residents
registered in the test. The theoretical battery autonomy in supervision
is about 36 hours. The results are limited to a single measurement
point, a sensor that does not measure volumetric water content,
thresholds not calibrated for the site and a battery that was only
calculated and simulated. It is concluded that it is possible to build,
with low-cost components and open-source technologies, an alert system
with every threshold grounded in the literature, which supports the
early warning of the population without replacing the dam's emergency
plan.
